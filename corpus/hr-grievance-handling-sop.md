# Grievance Handling SOP

## Purpose
This procedure explains how employees can raise a workplace concern and how
Employee Relations handles it. It aims to resolve concerns fairly, quickly and
confidentially, and to protect everyone involved from retaliation.

## Scope
The procedure covers concerns about working conditions, treatment by
colleagues or managers, workload, the application of HR policies, and
decisions that affect an employee's employment. It applies to full-time
employees, contract employees and interns.

Complaints of sexual harassment are handled by the Internal Committee under
the Prevention of Sexual Harassment policy, not under this procedure.
Concerns about fraud, bribery or other wrongdoing by the company should be
reported through the whistleblower channel.

## Principles
- **Confidentiality:** information is shared only with people who need it to
  resolve the concern.
- **No retaliation:** raising a concern in good faith never counts against an
  employee. Retaliation is a disciplinary matter.
- **Fairness:** everyone involved has a chance to give their account before a
  decision is made.
- **Right to be accompanied:** the employee may bring a colleague to any
  formal meeting.

## Stage one: informal resolution
Many concerns can be resolved by talking to the person involved or to the
employee's manager. Employees are encouraged to try this first where they feel
comfortable. Managers who receive a concern should listen, take notes, agree
next steps with the employee and follow up. If the concern is about the
manager, the employee may go directly to stage two.

## Stage two: formal grievance
1. The employee raises a *Confidential* ticket with the HR helpdesk, choosing
   the *Grievance* category, and describes the concern, the people involved
   and the outcome they are looking for.
2. Employee Relations acknowledges the ticket and assigns a case owner who has
   no involvement in the matter.
3. The case owner meets the employee to understand the concern and agrees
   whether an investigation is needed.
4. If an investigation is needed, the case owner or an appointed investigator
   interviews the people involved and reviews relevant documents, such as
   emails, chat records or HR system entries.
5. The case owner prepares a written outcome, setting out the findings, the
   decision and any actions, and meets the employee to explain it.
6. The outcome is recorded in the HR system. Only Employee Relations can see
   the case file.

## Grievances about HR policies
Where a concern is about how an HR policy has been applied, the case owner
first checks which version of the policy applies to the employee. Several HR
policies, including the Annual Leave Policy, the Work From Home Policy and the
Probation Policy, have more than one version in force, and the applicable
version depends on the employee's join date. Many policy disputes turn out to
be caused by the wrong version being applied; in that case the case owner
arranges for the record to be corrected and closes the grievance.

## Stage three: appeal
If the employee is not satisfied with the outcome, they may appeal in writing
through the same helpdesk ticket, explaining why. The appeal is heard by a
senior HR business partner who was not involved in the original decision,
together with a senior manager from another department. The appeal decision
is final within the company.

## Interim measures
While a grievance is being handled, Employee Relations may put interim
measures in place, such as a temporary change of reporting line or seating,
to protect the employee. Interim measures are not a finding against anyone.

## Timelines
Employee Relations aims to handle each stage promptly and keeps the employee
informed if a stage takes longer than expected, for example when witnesses
are unavailable.

## Records and data protection
Case files are kept confidential and retained in line with the company's data
retention schedule. Anyone involved in a grievance must not discuss it with
people outside the process.

## Support
Raising or being part of a grievance can be stressful. Employees can use the
employee assistance programme for free, confidential counselling at any
stage.

## Responsibilities
- **Employees:** raise concerns honestly and take part in the process.
- **Managers:** respond to informal concerns, cooperate with investigations
  and implement agreed actions.
- **Employee Relations:** run the process, keep records and report anonymised
  trends to the HR leadership team every quarter.
