# Personal Data Handling Guide

This guide builds on Data Protection Basics. It explains how to handle
personal data through its life cycle, from collection to deletion, and what
to check before starting a new project that uses it.

## Our obligations

We process personal data about employees, candidates, customers and the
people who work for our suppliers. Under India's Digital Personal Data
Protection Act and, for European customers, the GDPR, we must:

- have a lawful reason, such as consent or a contract, for each use;
- tell people clearly what we collect and why;
- keep the data accurate and secure;
- keep it no longer than needed;
- let people exercise their rights over it.

The Privacy team, part of Legal, is responsible for our privacy programme.
Every employee is responsible for handling data correctly in their own work.

## Collecting personal data

Before you collect personal data, confirm three things:

1. **Purpose.** Write down in one sentence why you need it.
2. **Minimum.** Remove every field you can do without. Optional fields are
   still collection.
3. **Notice.** Make sure the person sees our privacy notice, or a notice
   approved by Privacy, before or at the time you collect the data.

If you rely on consent, the consent must be a clear, separate action, such as
an unticked box. It must be as easy to withdraw as it was to give.

## Using and sharing personal data

- Use personal data only for the purpose you recorded. A new purpose needs a
  new check with Privacy.
- Access should follow least privilege. Ask for access only to the data your
  role needs, and give it up when you change roles.
- Share with other teams only on a need-to-know basis.
- Share with outside parties only under a signed contract that includes our
  data processing terms. Legal adds these terms during contract review.
- Never paste personal data into public AI tools, translation sites or
  online file converters.

## Transferring data outside India

Sending personal data to a supplier or colleague in another country is a
transfer. Transfers are allowed to most countries, but they must be listed
in the supplier's contract and in our processing records. Check with
Privacy before starting any new transfer.

## Keeping data secure

Follow the IT Security Basics FAQ. In particular:

- store personal data only in approved systems, never on personal devices;
- encrypt files that contain sensitive personal data before sending them;
- use access-controlled links rather than email attachments;
- lock your screen when you step away;
- print personal data only when you must, and collect prints at once.

## Retention and deletion

Keep personal data only for the period in the Records Retention Schedule.
Where the schedule says **Anonymise**, remove or irreversibly scramble every
field that could identify a person, including combinations such as postcode
plus date of birth. Test data must never be copied from production without
anonymisation.

## Individuals' rights

People can ask us to:

- tell them what personal data we hold and how we use it;
- correct inaccurate data;
- delete their data, where we have no reason to keep it;
- stop using it for direct marketing;
- name someone to exercise their rights if they die or become unable to.

Requests can arrive by any channel: email, chat, a support ticket or a
phone call. Whoever receives a request must forward it to Privacy through
the Legal portal on the same day, with the requester's contact details.
Privacy verifies the person's identity and coordinates the reply. Do not
confirm or deny what data we hold before Privacy has verified the request.

## Privacy impact assessments

A privacy impact assessment (PIA) is required before you:

- launch a new product or feature that uses personal data;
- start using a new supplier that will receive personal data;
- use personal data in a new way, such as for analytics or machine
  learning;
- monitor employees or customers in a new way.

Start the PIA in the Legal portal at the design stage, not just before
launch. Privacy reviews it and may require changes, such as shorter
retention or extra access controls.

## Data incidents

Report any suspected personal data breach at once in the Legal portal under
**Report a data incident**, even if you are not sure it is a breach. Do not
try to investigate or contact affected people yourself. The Privacy team
decides whether regulators and individuals must be notified.

## Contact
