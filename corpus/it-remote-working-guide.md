# Remote Working Technology Guide

This guide explains how to set up and look after your technology when you work
away from a company office, whether at home, at a client site or while
travelling. It covers equipment, connectivity, security and what to do when
something goes wrong.

It does not say how often you may work from home; that is set by the Work From
Home Policy. Nor does it restate the rules in the IT policies it refers to.
Where a rule comes from a policy, the policy is named so you can check the
version that applies to you.

## Your home workspace

### Equipment you can request
You can request accessories through the IT service portal. What you can
request, and where it is delivered, is set in the Laptop Request and Refresh
Policy. If you need something delivered to your home address, say so in the
request.

### Setting up your desk
- Place the monitor at arm's length with the top of the screen at eye level.
- Use an external keyboard and mouse if you work for long periods.
- Sit facing a window or a lamp, not with a window behind you, so that people
  can see you clearly on video calls.
- Use a headset for calls rather than the laptop speakers. It cuts echo and
  keeps conversations private.

### Personal equipment
You may use your own monitor, keyboard and mouse with your company laptop. Do
not connect personal storage devices such as USB drives or external hard
disks; they are blocked by device management. Do not use a personal computer
for company work.

## Getting connected

### Home internet
A stable broadband connection is enough for everyday work, including video
calls. If your Wi-Fi is unreliable, connect the laptop to your router with a
cable or move closer to the router. The company does not provide home
broadband.

### When you need the VPN
Which systems need the VPN, session limits, the rules on personal use and the
travel rules are all in the VPN Access Policy. Connect only when you need a
system that requires it. If you are disconnected or the VPN will not connect, see
the VPN Troubleshooting FAQ.

### Public Wi-Fi
Public networks in cafes, hotels and airports are not trusted. If you must
use one, connect to the VPN before doing any work, even on cloud applications,
and avoid entering passwords on shared screens. A hotspot from your phone is
safer than public Wi-Fi.

## Keeping data safe away from the office

- **Lock your screen** every time you step away, even at home.
- **Keep your laptop with you** when travelling. Never leave it in a car, even
  in the boot, and never check it in as hold luggage.
- **Watch who can see your screen** on trains, planes and in cafes. A privacy
  screen filter can be requested through the IT service portal.
- **Keep calls private.** Do not discuss confidential matters where you can be
  overheard.
- **Do not print confidential documents at home.** If you must print, use an
  office printer.
- **Store files in company storage**, not on the laptop's desktop or in
  personal cloud accounts. Files in company storage are backed up; files on
  the laptop are not.
- **Family and friends** must not use your company laptop, even briefly.

Passwords and multi-factor authentication work the same way at home as in the
office. The requirements are in the Password Rules policy, and the company
password manager is the only approved place to store work passwords.

## Keeping your laptop healthy

Your laptop receives security updates and software updates over the internet,
whether or not you are on the VPN. To keep it up to date:

- restart it at least once a week,
- let updates install when prompted rather than postponing them repeatedly,
- keep it connected to power and the internet at least once a day.

A laptop that has not checked in with device management for a long period
may lose access to company systems until it is updated.

## Working abroad

Working from another country for any period needs approval under the Work From
Home Policy, and VPN use abroad is covered by the travel section of the VPN
Access Policy; follow its notice requirements before you go. Carry the
laptop's charger and a plug adapter; power supplies are not replaced
overseas.

## When something goes wrong

### Hardware fault
If your laptop stops working while you are remote, call the IT service desk.
IT can send a loaner laptop by courier or arrange for you to collect one from
the nearest office. Faulty devices are returned by courier, which IT
arranges.

### Lost or stolen device
Report it by phone to the IT service desk as soon as you notice, and file a
police report if it was stolen. IT will lock and wipe the device remotely.
This is treated as a security incident under the IT Incident Escalation
Matrix.

### Software or access problems
Raise a ticket in the IT service portal. The IT Troubleshooting FAQ answers
the most common questions.

## Returning equipment

If you receive a replacement laptop, or when you leave the company, equipment
must be returned under the IT Asset Return Policy, which explains the return
options for employees who do not visit an office.
