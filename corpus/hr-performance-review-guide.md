# Performance Review Guide

This guide explains how the annual performance review works and what
employees and managers need to do at each step. It covers confirmed
employees. Reviews for employees still on probation follow the Probation
Policy instead.

## Why we review performance
Reviews give every employee a clear, fair picture of how they are doing,
recognise good work and agree where to grow next. They also feed into
decisions on pay increases and promotions, so they need to be done carefully
and on time.

## The cycle at a glance
1. **Goal setting** at the start of the year.
2. **Mid-year check-in** to review progress and adjust goals.
3. **Year-end review**, made up of a self-review, peer feedback and a manager
   review.
4. **Calibration** across teams.
5. **Review conversation** and sharing of the final rating.

People Operations announces the dates for each step on the employee portal.

## Step one: set goals
Employees draft goals in the HR system under *Performance* > *Goals*. Good
goals are specific, measurable and linked to the team's objectives. Most
employees have a small number of goals, each with a short description of
what success looks like. Managers review the draft, discuss it with the
employee and approve it. Goals can be changed during the year if priorities
change; record the change in the HR system so that the review reflects it.

## Step two: mid-year check-in
The mid-year check-in is a conversation, not a rating. The employee and
manager discuss progress against each goal, what support is needed and any
goals that should be updated. The manager records a short summary in the HR
system.

## Step three: year-end review

### Self-review
Employees write a self-review against each goal. Give concrete examples of
what you delivered and how, and be honest about what did not go well and what
you learned. Link to documents or dashboards where that helps.

### Peer feedback
Employees nominate colleagues who have worked closely with them to give
feedback. Managers may add or replace reviewers. Feedback should be specific,
balanced and focused on behaviour and outcomes, not personality. Peer
feedback is shared with the employee without the reviewer's name.

### Manager review
The manager reads the self-review and peer feedback, then writes an overall
assessment and proposes a rating. The rating scale and its descriptions are
shown in the HR system next to the rating field.

## Step four: calibration
Managers in each department meet to compare proposed ratings, so that similar
performance receives similar ratings across teams. Managers should come
prepared with evidence for each rating. Ratings may change during
calibration; the manager explains any change to the employee.

## Step five: review conversation
The manager and employee meet to discuss the review. The manager shares the
final rating, explains the reasons and agrees development goals for the next
year. The employee then acknowledges the review in the HR system and can add
comments. Acknowledging a review does not mean agreeing with it.

## Tips for employees
- Keep a simple record of achievements through the year so that the
  self-review is easy to write.
- Ask for feedback regularly rather than waiting for the year-end review.
- If you disagree with your rating, first talk to your manager. If that does
  not resolve it, you may raise it through the Grievance Handling SOP.

## Tips for managers
- Hold regular one-to-ones throughout the year so that nothing in the review
  is a surprise.
- Base ratings on evidence and outcomes, not on where or when the work was
  done. Employees working from home under the Work From Home Policy are
  assessed in the same way as those in the office.
- Watch for common biases, such as giving more weight to recent events or
  rating people like yourself more favourably.
- Do not discuss other employees' ratings.

## New joiners and people on leave
Employees who joined partway through the year are reviewed on the goals they
had time to work on. Employees who were on long leave during the year are
reviewed on the period they were at work, and their leave must not count
against them.

## Pay and promotion
Pay increases and promotions are decided after calibration and communicated
separately by the manager. Ratings are one input among several, including
role scope and market data.

## Help
For questions about the process or the HR system, raise a ticket with People
Operations through the HR helpdesk. Training sessions for managers are
offered before each year-end review.
