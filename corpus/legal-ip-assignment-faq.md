# Intellectual Property Assignment FAQ

Answers to common questions about the intellectual property (IP) clause in
your employment contract.

**What did I agree to when I signed my contract?**
Your contract assigns to the company the IP in anything you create in the
course of your job. This covers code, designs, documents, inventions and
data. You keep the right to be named as the author or inventor where the law
gives you that right.

**Does this cover things I make in my own time?**
Not usually. Work you do outside working hours, without company equipment or
confidential information, and that is not related to our business, stays
yours. If a side project is close to what the company does, declare it
before you start so there is no dispute later.

**How do I declare a side project?**
Use the conflict-of-interest declaration form in the Legal portal and choose
**other**. Describe the project and how it differs from your work here.
Legal replies in writing, and you should keep that reply.

**What about things I created before I joined?**
They stay yours. If you want to use any of them in your work here, list them
with Legal first. Otherwise it may be hard to show later that they existed
before you joined.

**Can I contribute to open-source projects?**
Yes, within the Open Source Contribution Guidelines published by
Engineering. Contributions made as part of your job need approval from your
engineering manager. Personal contributions to projects unrelated to our
products do not.

**Can I publish a paper or give a conference talk about my work?**
Yes, after review. Send the draft to Legal and your manager before you
submit it, so that we can check it does not reveal confidential information
or an invention we plan to patent.

**What happens if I invent something patentable?**
Tell your manager and submit an invention disclosure in the Legal portal.
Do not discuss it outside the company until Legal has decided whether to
file a patent. Inventors named on a filed patent receive a recognition award
under the Inventor Recognition Programme.

**Does the assignment continue after I leave?**
The assignment of work you created while employed does not end when you
leave. You must also keep confidential information confidential after you
leave.

**Who can I ask?**
Raise a question in the Legal portal under **IP and inventions**.
