# Conflict of Interest Guide

This guide explains what a conflict of interest is, how to spot one and what
to do about it. It applies to all employees, contractors and interns. For
the declaration form itself, see How to Declare a Conflict of Interest.

## What a conflict of interest is

A conflict of interest arises when your personal interests, or those of
people close to you, could influence, or look as if they influence, the
decisions you make for the company. Having a conflict is not wrong in
itself. Hiding one is.

The test is simple: if a colleague, customer or journalist learned about the
situation, would they reasonably question whether you acted in the
company's best interest? If the answer might be yes, declare it.

## Common situations

### Outside employment and directorships

You may take on outside work only if it does not compete with the company,
does not use company time, equipment or information, and does not affect
your performance. Paid or unpaid directorships of other companies always
need approval before you accept them. Directorships of non-profit bodies,
housing societies and resident welfare associations should still be
declared but are normally approved.

### Family and close relationships

Declare it if a family member or partner:

- works for a supplier, customer, competitor or partner you deal with;
- applies for a job in your team or a team you influence;
- reports to you, directly or indirectly.

You must not take part in hiring, promotion, pay or performance decisions
about a family member or partner. The Compliance team will arrange for
someone else to take those decisions.

### Financial interests

Declare any holding in a supplier, customer or competitor that is large
enough to matter to you or that gives you influence over that business.
Small holdings through mutual funds or index funds do not need to be
declared. If you hold shares in a listed company, also follow the Insider
Trading Code, which sets trading windows and pre-clearance rules.

### Gifts and hospitality

You may accept modest gifts and hospitality that are customary in business,
such as a branded notebook, a festival sweet box or a working lunch. You
must decline, or hand over to Compliance:

- cash or cash equivalents, such as gift cards and vouchers, of any value;
- any gift worth more than INR 5,000;
- anything offered while a tender, contract renewal or negotiation with the
  giver is open;
- travel or accommodation paid by a supplier, unless Compliance has approved
  it in advance.

Record every gift worth more than a token amount in the gifts register in
the Legal portal, including those you decline. Giving gifts to customers or
public officials follows the Anti-Bribery Policy, which is stricter.

### Using company opportunities

If you learn about a business opportunity through your job, it belongs to
the company. Do not take it for yourself, or pass it to friends or family,
without written approval.

## What to do when you spot a conflict

1. **Declare it.** Use the form in the Legal portal. Declare as soon as you
   become aware of the conflict, not when a decision is due.
2. **Step back.** Until the declaration is reviewed, do not take part in
   decisions that the conflict could affect. Tell the people you work with
   that you have stepped back, without needing to give details.
3. **Follow the agreed action.** Your manager and Compliance will record one
   of the following:
   - *No action needed.* The conflict is noted and you carry on.
   - *Managed.* You carry on with safeguards, such as a second approver on
     decisions involving the other party.
   - *Removed.* Someone else takes over the relationship or decision.
   - *Not permitted.* You must end the outside activity or interest.
4. **Update it.** If your situation changes, submit a new declaration. The
   annual compliance certification also asks you to confirm that your
   declarations are up to date.

## Confidentiality

Declarations are seen only by your manager, the Compliance team and, where
needed, HR. They are not shared with your wider team.

## Consequences of not declaring

Failing to declare a conflict is treated as a breach of the Code of Conduct
and can lead to disciplinary action, even if the undeclared conflict did not
actually change any decision. Contracts or hires made under an undeclared
conflict may be reviewed and reversed.

## Examples

- *A developer's sister-in-law is the account manager at a cloud supplier
  the team is evaluating.* Declare. The developer can keep working on the
  technical evaluation but should not sit on the selection panel.
- *A finance analyst is invited by a bank to a weekend resort event.*
  Decline, or ask Compliance for approval before accepting.
- *A designer sells illustrations on a personal website in their spare
  time.* No declaration needed if the work is unrelated to our products and
  done on personal equipment.
