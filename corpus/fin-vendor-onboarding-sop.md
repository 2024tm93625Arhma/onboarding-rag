# Vendor Onboarding SOP

## Purpose
This procedure sets out how a new supplier is checked and set up in the
procurement system before the company buys from it. It protects the company
against fraud, sanctions and tax risk, and makes sure suppliers can be paid
correctly. Employees who want to use a new vendor should follow the Checklist
for Onboarding a New Vendor; this SOP describes what happens behind it.

## Scope
All suppliers of goods and services, including individual consultants and
freelancers. It does not cover employees being reimbursed for expenses, or
customers.

## Roles
- **Requester:** the employee who wants to use the vendor. Raises the request
  and keeps the vendor moving.
- **Budget holder:** confirms there is a business need and budget.
- **Procurement analyst:** reviews documents, runs checks and approves the
  vendor record.
- **IT Security:** reviews vendors who will access company systems or data.
- **Accounts Payable:** confirms bank and tax details are complete before the
  first payment.

## Procedure

### 1. Request
1. The requester searches the vendor master to make sure the supplier does not
   already exist. Duplicate vendor records are a common route for duplicate
   payments.
2. The requester raises a *New Vendor Request* with the legal name, contact,
   category and estimated annual spend.
3. The budget holder approves the request in the system.
4. The system sends the vendor an invitation to the supplier portal.

### 2. Vendor documents
The vendor uploads the following through the supplier portal:
- PAN card
- GST registration certificate, or a declaration of non-registration
- Cancelled cheque or a bank letter on the bank's letterhead
- MSME (Udyam) registration certificate, if applicable
- Signed Supplier Code of Conduct
- Certificate of incorporation, for companies
- Information security questionnaire, for vendors who will handle company
  data or connect to company systems

Procurement does not accept documents sent by email. If a vendor cannot use
the portal, the analyst records the reason and uploads the documents on their
behalf.

### 3. Checks
The procurement analyst:
1. Validates the PAN and GSTIN on the government portals and confirms that the
   names match the legal name.
2. Screens the vendor and its directors against the sanctions and debarment
   lists.
3. Checks for conflicts of interest: whether any employee or their close
   relative owns or works for the vendor. The requester must declare any
   known relationship on the request.
4. Confirms MSME status, because MSME suppliers must be paid within the
   period set by law and are flagged in the payment run.
5. Sends the security questionnaire to IT Security where one is required.
   The vendor cannot be approved until IT Security signs it off.

### 4. Bank verification
The analyst calls the vendor on a phone number taken from an independent
source, such as the vendor's website or the GST portal, not from the
documents supplied. The analyst confirms the account number and the name of
the account holder, and records the call in the vendor record. A bank account
in a name different from the vendor's legal name is rejected.

### 5. Approval and set-up
Once all checks pass, the analyst creates the vendor record, sets the payment
terms and approves it. Standard payment terms are 45 days from receipt of a
valid invoice unless a contract says otherwise. A second Procurement analyst
reviews the record before it becomes active. The requester is notified with
the vendor number.

The target is to complete onboarding within 10 working days of the vendor
uploading a complete set of documents.

## Changes to vendor records
Vendors request changes to their address, contact or bank details through the
supplier portal. Every bank change is verified by a call-back, as in step 4,
and reviewed by a second analyst. Requests to change bank details by email or
phone are never actioned, and are reported to the Finance Controller as
possible fraud.

## Inactive vendors
Vendors with no purchase order or payment for 18 months are blocked
automatically. To use one again, the requester raises a reactivation request
and the vendor re-confirms its documents and bank details.

## Records
All documents, check results and call-back notes are kept in the vendor
record for eight years after the vendor is last used.

## Exceptions
Any exception to this procedure, such as an urgent payment to a vendor before
checks are complete, needs written approval from the Finance Controller and is
reported to the CFO each month.

## Contact
Procurement, through a Finance ticket on the employee portal.
