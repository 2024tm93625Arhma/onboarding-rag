# Procure-to-Pay Guide

This guide explains how the company buys goods and services from suppliers,
from the first quote to the final payment. It is for any employee who needs to
buy something for their team. The step-by-step screens are covered in How to
Raise a Purchase Order; this guide explains the process around them and why
each step exists.

## Principles
- **No PO, no pay.** Every purchase from a supplier needs an approved purchase
  order before the order is placed. The only exception is a small purchase of
  up to INR 10,000 paid on a corporate card.
- **Separation of duties.** The person who asks for a purchase does not
  approve it, and neither of them pays the invoice. Procurement places orders
  and Accounts Payable makes payments.
- **Approved vendors only.** Orders can be placed only with vendors that have
  completed onboarding. See the Vendor Onboarding SOP.
- **Budget first.** A requisition is checked against the cost centre budget
  when it is submitted. If there is not enough budget left, the budget holder
  must agree a transfer with Finance before it can be approved.

## The process at a glance
1. Identify the need and check whether an existing contract covers it.
2. Get quotes.
3. Raise a purchase requisition.
4. The requisition is approved.
5. Procurement issues the purchase order.
6. The supplier delivers.
7. You record a goods receipt.
8. The supplier sends an invoice.
9. Accounts Payable matches and pays it.

## 1. Check existing contracts
Before you look for a new supplier, search the contract register in the
procurement system. The company has framework agreements for common
categories such as laptops and accessories, office supplies, event venues,
courier services and recruitment agencies. Buying under a framework agreement
is faster because prices are already agreed and no quotes are needed.

## 2. Get quotes
| Order value | Quotes needed |
|---|---|
| Up to INR 5,00,000 | One written quote |
| Above INR 5,00,000 | Three written quotes, or a single-source justification |

A single-source justification explains why only one supplier can do the work,
for example because they own the software being supported. It must be signed
by the department head. Do not split one purchase into several smaller orders
to stay under a threshold; Finance reviews orders for splitting every quarter.

## 3. Raise a purchase requisition
In the procurement system, create a requisition with the supplier, a clear
description, line items, delivery dates, the cost centre and the quotes
attached. Be specific: *12 x 27-inch monitors for the Bengaluru design team*
is better than *monitors*.

## 4. Approval
Requisitions are routed automatically:

| Order value | Approvers |
|---|---|
| Up to INR 5,00,000 | Budget holder for the cost centre |
| Above INR 5,00,000 up to INR 25,00,000 | Budget holder and department head |
| Above INR 25,00,000 | Budget holder, department head and CFO |

Purchases of software, cloud services or anything that will hold company data
also go to IT Security for review, whatever the value.

## 5. Purchase order
Once approved, Procurement checks the requisition, converts it to a PO and
sends it to the supplier. Procurement may negotiate on price or terms before
sending. You get a notification with the PO number. Pass it to your supplier
contact and ask them to quote it on every invoice.

## 6. Delivery
Goods are delivered to the address on the PO, usually the office reception.
Reception signs for the delivery and lets you know. For services, delivery
means the work agreed in the PO has been done.

## 7. Goods receipt
Record a goods receipt in the procurement system as soon as you have checked
what arrived. Enter the quantity actually received. If only part of the order
has arrived, receive that part now and the rest later. The goods receipt is
your confirmation that the company got what it ordered, and without it
Accounts Payable cannot pay.

## 8. Invoice
The supplier sends the invoice to the Accounts Payable inbox shown on the PO.
Invoices sent to individual employees are not logged. See the Supplier
Invoice FAQ for what an invoice must contain.

## 9. Matching and payment
Accounts Payable performs a three-way match between the PO, the goods receipt
and the invoice. If they agree within a small tolerance, the invoice is
approved for payment. If not, it is put on hold and the requester is
notified. Standard payment terms are 45 days from the date AP receives a valid
invoice, and payments are released in the weekly supplier payment run on
Thursday.

## Closing a PO
When everything has been delivered and invoiced, the PO closes automatically.
If you no longer need the remaining lines, ask Procurement to close it so the
unused budget is released.

## Getting help
Raise a Finance ticket on the employee portal and choose *Procurement* for
questions about quotes, requisitions or suppliers, or *Accounts Payable* for
invoices and payments.
