# Disciplinary Procedure for Managers

**Classification: Confidential. Managers at G6 and above, and HR.**

This procedure tells managers how to handle suspected misconduct. It does
not cover poor performance, which is handled through the performance
improvement plan, or complaints raised by an employee, which follow the
Grievance Handling SOP. Always involve Employee Relations before taking any
formal step.

## Categories of misconduct

- **Minor misconduct:** lateness, misuse of company equipment, breaches of
  office rules. Usually handled informally first.
- **Serious misconduct:** repeated minor misconduct, insubordination,
  negligence that causes loss, breaches of the Information Classification
  Standard.
- **Gross misconduct:** theft, fraud, violence, harassment, working under
  the influence of alcohol or drugs, deliberate data leaks. Can lead to
  dismissal for a first offence.

## Steps

1. **Initial review.** The manager and Employee Relations decide whether a
   formal process is needed.
2. **Show-cause notice.** The employee receives a written notice setting
   out the allegation and has 72 hours to reply in writing.
3. **Investigation.** An investigating officer at least one grade above the
   employee, and outside their reporting line, gathers evidence. The
   investigation must finish within 20 working days of the notice.
4. **Hearing.** The employee may bring a colleague. The hearing is minuted
   and the minutes are shared with the employee within 3 working days.
5. **Outcome.** The decision is given in writing within 5 working days of
   the hearing.

## Suspension

For suspected gross misconduct, the employee may be suspended pending
investigation with approval from the Head of Employee Relations.
Suspension is not a penalty. During suspension the employee receives a
subsistence allowance of 50 percent of fixed pay for the first 90 days and
75 percent after that. If the allegation is not proven, the balance is paid
in full.

## Sanctions and how long they last

| Sanction | Stays on file for |
|---|---|
| Verbal warning (recorded) | 180 days |
| Written warning | 12 months |
| Final written warning | 24 months |
| Dismissal | Permanent |

An employee with a live written or final written warning is not eligible
for promotion and receives a rating multiplier of zero for variable pay in
that cycle.

## Appeals

The employee may appeal within 7 working days of the outcome. The appeal is
heard by a manager at least two grades above the original decision maker
who was not involved in the case. The appeal decision is final.

## Records

Employee Relations keeps the case file. Managers must not keep their own
copies of notes, statements or evidence after the case closes.
