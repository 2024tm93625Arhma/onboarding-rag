# Data Protection Basics

A short introduction to handling personal data at work. It applies to
everyone, whatever your role.

## What counts as personal data

Personal data is any information about an identifiable living person. That
includes obvious items such as names, phone numbers and email addresses, and
less obvious ones such as employee IDs, IP addresses, photos, voice
recordings and location data.

Some personal data is **sensitive** and needs extra care: health
information, bank and card details, government ID numbers, caste or
religion, sexual orientation and biometric data.

## Five rules to follow

1. **Collect only what you need.** Do not ask for a date of birth if an age
   check is enough.
2. **Use it only for the reason it was collected.** Customer support data is
   not a marketing list.
3. **Keep it in approved systems.** Do not copy personal data into personal
   drives, chat messages or unapproved AI tools.
4. **Share it only with people who need it.** Check the recipient before you
   send a file, and prefer links with access control over attachments.
5. **Delete it when it is no longer needed.** Follow the Records Retention
   Schedule.

## Requests from individuals

People can ask to see, correct or delete the personal data we hold about
them. If a customer, candidate or colleague makes such a request to you, do
not answer it yourself. Forward it to the Privacy team through the Legal
portal on the same day.

## If something goes wrong

A personal data breach is any loss, leak or unauthorised access, such as an
email sent to the wrong person or a lost unencrypted USB drive. Report it at
once in the Legal portal under **Report a data incident**. Early reporting
matters because some breaches must be notified to a regulator within a short
legal deadline. You will not be blamed for reporting honestly.
